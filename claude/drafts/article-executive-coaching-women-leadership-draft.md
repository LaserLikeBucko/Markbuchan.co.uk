### Article body

A lot of leadership coaching aimed at women starts from a quiet assumption: that the gap between where you are and where you're trying to get to is a confidence gap, a negotiation gap, an assertiveness gap — something to close by becoming more of something. Louder. Bolder. More visibly strategic.

Sometimes that's true. More often, in the coaching room, it isn't the whole story.

**The double bind is real, and naming it isn't complaining**

Well-documented research on gender and leadership perception describes a genuine bind: behaviour read as "decisive" in a male leader is frequently read as "difficult" in a female one. The same directness that gets one person promoted gets another person quietly noted as "not quite a culture fit." This isn't a personal failing to coach away. It's a real feature of a lot of organisational environments, and pretending it isn't there doesn't help anyone navigate it.

What coaching can do is different from "fix your confidence." It's helping you see clearly which parts of what you're experiencing are about your own alignment — where your actual values and your daily behaviour have drifted apart under pressure — and which parts are about a system responding to you in ways that have nothing to do with your competence. Those require different responses, and conflating them usually leads to more of the wrong kind of effort.

**The performance tax**

Many senior women describe a specific, exhausting pattern: constantly managing how they're perceived alongside actually doing the job. Softening a direct point so it doesn't land as aggressive. Doing the emotional labour of keeping a room comfortable while also being the person responsible for the hard decision in it. Being the one who remembers birthdays and smooths conflict, on top of the actual role.

That's not a soft-skills bonus. It's a second, unpaid job running in parallel with the first one — and it's a significant, underacknowledged driver of exhaustion at the executive level. If that pattern isn't named, it gets absorbed as "this is just what leadership feels like for me," which quietly becomes its own hidden gap: performing a version of leadership you didn't choose, because naming the alternative felt riskier than the cost of not naming it.

**What this coaching actually addresses**

Using the same VIM (Values Integrity Model), ECHO framework and Inner Coach methodology from *The Hidden Gap*, the work isn't about adding a confidence layer on top of an unexamined system. It's about getting precise: where is the gap actually located? In your own values and behaviour under pressure? In a system that's responding to you inconsistently regardless of what you do? Usually, it's some combination — and knowing which parts are yours to work with and which parts need a different kind of response (a conversation with the organisation, a boundary, sometimes a decision to leave) is the actual outcome of good coaching here, not a generic confidence boost.

The leaders I've worked with in this position don't typically need to be told to speak up more. They need someone to help them see, with real precision, where they've been quietly overriding their own judgement to manage how they're perceived — and what becomes possible once that override stops being automatic.

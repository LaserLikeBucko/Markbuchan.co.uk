### RECOGNITION SECTION (white)

**Eyebrow (red):** Who this is for

**Headline:** You already know something has shifted.

Decisions that used to take seconds now take days. Sunday evening brings a dread you can't quite name. You've stopped reading the things you used to devour, and started over-filling your calendar so there's no space left to feel what's underneath it. You'd call it a performance dip if you had to name it — because performance is the language you know.

**Pull quote (red border-left, matches existing pattern):**
The same drive that built your success is what stops you from reading exhaustion as a signal. That's the trap — and it isn't solved by pushing harder.

That gap — between what you're capable of and what you're currently able to access — doesn't close because you try harder. It closes because you see what's actually driving it.

### THREE SCENARIOS (dark `#111110` section, matches existing "does this sound familiar" pattern)

**Eyebrow (yellow):** Does any of this sound familiar?

**Headline (yellow):** Leaders I work with often say one of these things...

**Card 1:**
"I used to love this. Now I just get through it."
The exhaustion isn't physical — it's the quiet disappearance of meaning from work you built your identity around. That's not weakness. That's a signal worth taking seriously.

**Card 2:**
"If I stop, everything falls apart."
Delegation resistance in burned-out leaders is rarely about control. It's about identity — you built your career on being the person who handles everything. Handing work off can feel like losing yourself, not just losing tasks.

**Card 3:**
"Everyone thinks I'm fine. I'm not entirely sure I am."
High-functioning burnout is real and it's common at this level. You can be performing well by every external measure and still be running on a system that's quietly failing underneath.

### WHAT SHIFTS (three outcomes, matches `/coaching` "01 02 03" pattern)

**Eyebrow (red):** What changes

**01 — Recognition.** What's been driving the exhaustion — the gap between your declared values and what's actually running the show under pressure — gets named and made visible, instead of staying underground as vague dread.

**02 — Redesign.** Concrete change: what only you can do versus what you've been holding out of habit. Boundaries that match your actual operating environment, not generic wellness advice.

**03 — Sustainable capacity.** Not working less for its own sake — working with real awareness of what you have and making conscious trade-offs, so the same pattern doesn't reassert itself in six months.

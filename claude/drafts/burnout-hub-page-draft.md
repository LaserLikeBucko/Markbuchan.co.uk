### RECOGNITION SECTION (white background, mirrors "You already know something is off" pattern from `/coaching`)

**Eyebrow:** Who this is for

**Headline:** You already know something isn't sustainable.

Maybe it's the Sunday-night dread that starts earlier every week. The decisions that used to take seconds and now take days. The sense that you're performing competence rather than feeling it. Or maybe it's not you — it's watching your best people quietly disappear into exhaustion, one by one, while the org chart says everything is fine.

Burnout rarely announces itself with a name. It shows up first as *"I just need to get sharper"* or *"I'll rest when this quarter is done."* By the time it has a name, it's usually been building for a while.

**Pull quote (border-left red accent, matches existing pattern):**
Burnout is what happens when the gap between what's declared and what's actually happening gets wide enough for long enough.

### RESEARCH CALLOUT (white background — new structural element for this site, borrowed as a device from competitor research, not content)

**Eyebrow (red):** What the data says

**Headline:** Even the research agrees — this was never just about workload.

Gallup's 2025–2026 *State of the Global Workplace* research puts it plainly: burnout splits roughly 50/50 between the work itself (workload, tasks, time pressure) and the people-and-culture side of work — management, fairness, belonging, relationships. Employees who experience unfair treatment are over twice as likely to report high burnout. Employees who feel a genuine sense of belonging report markedly lower stress.

That 50/50 split is, in plainer language, exactly what *The Hidden Gap* has been describing for organisations, and exactly what Catherine has seen and lived through personally. The workload matters. So does the gap between what an organisation says it values and what it actually rewards day to day. Fixing only one side doesn't hold.

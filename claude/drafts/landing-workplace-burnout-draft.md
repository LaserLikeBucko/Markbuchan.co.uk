### WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWS (research callout — new structural device)

**Eyebrow (red):** What the data says

**Headline:** It's not just workload. It was never just workload.

Gallup's 2025–2026 workplace research puts real numbers on something that's usually treated as a vague cultural complaint:

- Burnout splits roughly 50/50 between workload/time pressure and the people-and-culture side of work — management quality, fairness, belonging.
- Employees who experience unfair treatment are more than twice as likely to report high burnout.
- Manager burnout is rising sharply — and burned-out managers have less capacity to support their teams, creating a cascade that spreads rather than staying contained.
- A genuine sense of belonging roughly halves reported stress levels. Isolation — worse in a lot of remote and hybrid setups — does the opposite.

Half the problem is how much work there is. The other half is whether the values your organisation declares match the ones actually driving decisions when things get hard. That second half is where most interventions stop looking — and it's exactly where the hidden gap lives.

---

### THE MANAGER CASCADE (dark `#111110` section — makes the systemic point concrete)

**Eyebrow (yellow):** The pattern underneath

**Headline (yellow):** It rarely starts with the person who looks burned out.

It usually starts one or two levels up. A manager absorbing pressure from above without the support or clarity to pass anything down cleanly. That manager burns out quietly, becomes less available, less patient, less able to advocate for their team — and the team feels the absence of support long before anyone names what's actually happening.

By the time burnout is visible in an individual contributor, it's often already been running in the system for months. Treating the visible symptom without addressing where it started is why so many wellness initiatives don't hold.

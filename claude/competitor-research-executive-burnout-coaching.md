# Competitor Research: Executive Burnout Coaching

Reviewed the top organic (non-sponsored) UK Google results for "executive burnout coaching" on 9 Jul 2026, plus adjacent pages for "executive wellbeing" / "leadership burnout." This is a synthesis of patterns and themes — paraphrased and analyzed, not copied. Use it to inform tone, structure, and differentiation for `/executive-burnout-coaching`, not as a template to clone.

## Sites reviewed

- **Tandem Coaching** (tandemcoach.co) — long-form educational blog post, research/data-driven
- **Mrs Mindset** (mrsmindset.co.uk) — solo therapist brand, urgency-driven
- **Emma Draycott** (emmadraycott.co.uk) — B2B/HR-facing, named signature methodology
- **Balanceology** (balanceology.uk) — training-institute/authority positioning, founder-led

## Positioning patterns observed

Four distinct angles show up across the field, and none of them frame burnout the way Mark's existing IP does:

1. **Systemic/behavioral framing** (Tandem) — burnout as an occupational phenomenon (citing the WHO's ICD-11 classification and Christina Maslach's research on organisational mismatches), not a personal failing. Positions coaching as addressing the system-and-self relationship, not just individual resilience.
2. **Urgency/personal-therapist framing** (Mrs Mindset) — fear-based language ("don't wait until it's too late"), symptom lists, personal 1:1 brand.
3. **B2B ROI framing** (Emma Draycott) — pitched at the organisation/HR buyer, not the burned-out leader directly. Heavy use of external stats (retention cost, productivity loss) and a named proprietary method.
4. **Institute/authority framing** (Balanceology) — founder credentials (MCC, ICF-accredited), "UK Institute for Burnout Coaching," single powerful outcome stat, corporate client testimonial.

**The gap:** none of them frame burnout as a *values-alignment* problem — the gap between what someone (or an organisation) declares as important and what's actually driving behaviour under pressure. That's Mark's authentic angle already, via the Hidden Gap thesis, and it's genuinely differentiated from anything in this set. It shouldn't be diluted to sound more like these competitors — if anything, lean into it harder, since it's a real point of difference, not just a marketing angle.

## Structural patterns worth adopting

- **Personal vignette opener.** Tandem opens with a short client scene before any explanation ("she said X, not Y — she was slower, not burned out"). This is exactly the device Mark already uses in "From the trenches" sections on `/coaching` and `/work-with-me` — strong validation that the existing pattern is right; just needs a burnout-specific vignette.
- **A comparison table.** Tandem uses a coaching-vs-therapy-vs-organisational-change table to directly answer "is this the right fit for me?" and "when should I refer out?" This is a structural device the rest of Mark's site doesn't currently use anywhere, and it would do real work on a burnout page specifically, since "is this a coaching problem or a clinical one?" is the natural hesitation for this topic.
- **A phased timeline.** Tandem breaks recovery into stages (stabilisation → redesign → consolidation) with rough week/month markers. Makes an abstract process concrete and answers "how long does this take?" without needing an FAQ.
- **External stats/citations for credibility.** Every single competitor reviewed leans on outside research (WHO, JAMA trial, Maslach, Deloitte, McKinsey) rather than only their own claims. Mark's site currently doesn't cite external research anywhere — even one or two well-chosen stats (with sources) on a burnout page would match the credibility bar this niche expects.
- **A named methodology, called out visually.** Emma Draycott's "Freedom Formula™" and Balanceology's "Burnout to Brilliance Method" both get their own visual treatment as a distinct "system," not just mentioned in passing. Mark already has this — VIM, ECHO, Inner Coach — but it currently reads as supporting detail on `/coaching` rather than a named framework with its own presence. Worth a dedicated "the method" section on the burnout page with the same visual weight competitors give their own systems.
- **A "who this is for" recognition section.** Every competitor has some version of this (Emma Draycott's "Who I Help," Tandem's symptom list). Matches the section Mark's `/coaching` page already does well ("You already know something is off") — reuse that pattern, burnout-specific.
- **FAQ section near the end.** Tandem's is the most developed — direct questions like "how long does coaching for burnout take?" Worth including on the new page; several FAQ answers can double as content for supporting blog posts.

## CTA patterns

- **Universally soft, low-commitment first step.** Nobody says "buy" or "sign up" — it's "book a free/complimentary consultation," "book a private consultation call." This matches Mark's existing "Book a Diagnostic Conversation" CTA pattern exactly — no change needed there, it's already aligned with what converts in this niche.
- **Repeated, not single.** Mrs Mindset repeats the same CTA three times down one page; Tandem places one mid-article and one at the end. Mark's existing pages typically have it at the very end only (plus a hero CTA) — consider adding one more mid-page CTA on the new burnout page, positioned right after the "who this is for" section, where recognition peaks.
- **Framed as a question or low-risk next step**, never hard-sell language — consistent with Mark's existing brand voice already.

## Recommendations for `/executive-burnout-coaching`

1. Keep the values-alignment framing as the headline differentiator — don't drift toward generic "burnout is systemic" or "burnout is trauma" language that just echoes competitors.
2. Open with a short client vignette (matching existing "From the trenches" pattern), burnout-specific.
3. Add a "the method" section giving VIM/ECHO/Inner Coach the same dedicated visual treatment competitors give their named systems — this is free differentiation since Mark already has real IP behind it (the book) that most competitors don't.
4. Add one comparison element addressing "is this coaching, or do I need therapy / organisational change?" — directly borrowed as a structural idea (not content) from Tandem, adapted to Mark's voice and the VIM framework.
5. Include a simple phased outline of what changes over time, consistent with the diagnostic/programme structure already used on `/coaching`.
6. Pull in 1–2 credible external stats on executive burnout (WHO/ICD-11 classification, or similar) with attribution, since every competitor in this space uses outside data and Mark's site currently doesn't.
7. Keep the existing single-CTA-style Calendly link, but add one earlier instance of it after the recognition section, not just at the end.

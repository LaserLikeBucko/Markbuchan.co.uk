## New research: Gallup 2025–2026 workplace data (via Grok)

Key findings from recent workplace burnout research (Gallup *State of the Global Workplace* and similar 2025–2026 studies), summarized:

- **Overall pattern — the headline finding:** burnout splits roughly 50/50 between "the work itself" (workload, tasks) and "people/culture" (management, fairness, relationships, belonging). This is close to an independent empirical validation of the Hidden Gap thesis — burnout isn't purely a workload problem, it's equally a values/culture-gap problem. Strong hook for a flagship article: research validates what the book has been saying.
- **Workload & time pressure** — top driver (~35% cite as primary stress driver). Notably, AI often *increases* burnout rather than reducing it: frequent AI users show higher burnout than non-users (45% vs 35% in some studies) — "AI brain fry" / cognitive fatigue from correcting AI errors and taking on more tasks rather than fewer. This is current (2025–2026) and none of the competitor pages reviewed are talking about it yet — a genuine white-space content angle.
- **Poor management & leadership gaps** — unclear communication, lack of support, and unfair treatment (employees experiencing unfair treatment are 2.3x more likely to report high burnout). Manager burnout itself is rising, creating a cascade: burned-out managers provide less support, which burns out their teams. Directly relevant to Mark's actual audience (leaders and managers, not just individual sufferers) and maps onto the Values Integrity Model (fairness as a values dimension).
- **Lack of belonging/psychological safety** — isolation (worse in remote/hybrid setups), lack of belonging roughly doubles stress rates, low autonomy/control.
- **Broader pressures** — job insecurity, financial stress, blurred boundaries/digital overload, toxic culture (cited by up to 80% in some surveys), rapid change including poorly-supported AI rollouts.
- **Emerging themes** — AI-related anxiety (fear of displacement, prominent in tech), lingering post-pandemic/economic uncertainty, generational differences (under-35s report higher stress).
- Gallup's own framing: it's highly preventable through better work design, manager enablement, and supportive leadership — far more effective than individual wellness perks alone. How employees *experience* workload and management matters more than raw hours.
